    EMI Calculator guide

    The EMI formula

    EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. This is the standard reducing-balance method banks use: interest each month is charged only on what you still owe, which is why the interest share of your EMI shrinks month after month (visible in the amortization summary above).

    Worked example: ₹10,00,000 at 9% for 5 years

    Stretch the same loan to a longer tenure and the EMI falls — but since the balance stays high for longer, the total interest climbs sharply. Always compare total interest, not just the monthly EMI, when two offers differ in tenure.

    Three honest ways to pay less interest

    • Shorter tenure: the biggest lever. Pick the shortest tenure whose EMI you can pay comfortably even in a bad month.
    • Prepay principal early: a lump sum paid in the first third of the loan kills far more future interest than the same amount paid near the end — check your lender’s prepayment charges first.
    • Compare the full cost, not the rate alone: processing fees, bundled insurance and “flat rate” quotes (which are not reducing-balance) can make a lower-rate loan more expensive.

    Frequently asked questions

    What does EMI stand for?

    Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay the lender every month. Each EMI contains both interest and principal; early instalments are mostly interest, later ones mostly principal.

    Does a longer tenure save money?

    No. A longer tenure lowers your monthly EMI but increases the total interest paid — sometimes by a lot. Compare the “total interest” figure for two tenures above before deciding what you can afford.

    Can my EMI change after the loan starts?

    On fixed-rate loans, no. On floating-rate loans (most home loans), the rate moves with the lender’s benchmark, and the bank either changes your EMI or extends/shortens your tenure. This calculator assumes one constant rate for the whole tenure.
