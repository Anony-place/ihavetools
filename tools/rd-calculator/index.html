    RD Calculator guide

    How RD maturity is calculated

    Indian banks compound RD interest quarterly. This calculator uses the standard closed form — A = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^(−1/3))] — where P is your monthly deposit, i is the quarterly rate (annual rate ÷ 400) and n is the number of quarters, assuming each instalment lands at the start of its month. That is why the same monthly amount earns noticeably less in an RD than the full sum would in an FD: the last instalment is invested for barely a month.

    Worked example: ₹5,000 a month at 7%

    RD vs FD vs SIP — pick by the job

    • Goal in 6–24 months, amount must be exact (fees, a gadget, a trip): RD — guaranteed, no market risk.
    • Lump sum already in hand: FD earns more than drip-feeding the same money into an RD.
    • Goal 5+ years away and you can tolerate ups and downs: a mutual-fund SIP has historically beaten RD rates, but nothing about it is guaranteed.
    • Tax: RD and FD interest is taxed every year at your slab rate — at the 30% slab, a 7% RD effectively returns under 5% after tax.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is RD interest calculated?

    Each monthly instalment earns interest compounded quarterly for the time it stays invested — early instalments earn the most. Calculators use the standard closed-form formula A = P × [((1+i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ (1 − (1+i)^(−1/3))], where i is the quarterly rate. Your bank’s exact figure can differ by a few rupees depending on the actual deposit dates in each quarter.

    Is RD interest taxable?

    Yes. RD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, and the bank deducts TDS when the interest crosses the prescribed yearly threshold. Unlike PPF, there is no tax exemption on RD interest.

    What is the difference between an RD and an FD?

    An FD is one lump sum locked in once; an RD builds the deposit month by month from your salary. Because later RD instalments earn interest for less time, depositing the same total earns less interest in an RD than in an FD of the same rate and overall period.
