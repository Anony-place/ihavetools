    How to use Pixels to Inches Converter

    1. Choose the conversion direction and enter a pixel or inch value.
    2. Enter the intended pixels per inch (PPI).
    3. Convert and check the physical units or rounded pixel count.

    Pixels to Inches Converter guide

    Pixels to physical size

    Divide pixels by the intended PPI. At 300 pixels per inch, 3000 pixels span 10 inches, or 25.4 centimetres. The same pixels at 150 PPI span 20 inches.

    Physical size to pixels

    Multiply the size in inches by PPI. A 10-inch edge at 300 PPI needs 3000 pixels. This calculator rounds a fractional pixel count to the nearest whole pixel and shows the unrounded value.

    Understand the assumptions

    • PPI is image pixels per inch; printer DPI describes ink dots per inch and is not necessarily the same.
    • Physical size depends on print settings, scaling and device density. CSS pixels are reference units and do not necessarily match physical screen pixels.
    • A PPI choice is not a promise of visual quality. Displayed values use floating-point arithmetic and are rounded.
    • Enter 1–1 billion whole pixels, or positive inches up to 1 billion; PPI must be positive and at most 1 million. Decimal inputs support up to six fractional places.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many inches is 3000 pixels at 300 PPI?

    3000 ÷ 300 is 10 inches, equivalent to 25.4 centimetres.

    Is 300 PPI always required?

    No. Suitable resolution depends on print size, content, viewing distance and printer requirements. Check with the print provider.

    Are PPI and DPI the same?

    No. PPI describes image pixels per inch; DPI describes printer dots per inch. They are often used loosely but are different quantities.

    Will this tell me the physical size on any screen?

    No. Screen scaling, CSS reference pixels and physical device density can differ from the entered PPI.
