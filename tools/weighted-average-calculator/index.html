    Weighted Average Calculator guide

    Weighted mean formula

    Weighted mean = Σ(value × weight) ÷ Σ(weight). Larger weights have greater influence. Weights may be counts, credit hours or percentages; use the same meaning consistently.

    Worked example

    For scores 80 and 90 with weights 3 and 2, the weighted sum is 80 × 3 + 90 × 2 = 420. Total weight is 5, so the weighted average is 84.

    Avoid common mistakes

    • Do not average the weights; divide the weighted sum by their total.
    • Use a non-negative weight for each value and at least one positive weight.
    • The result is arithmetic, not a course policy: confirm how your institution applies credits, rounding and exclusions.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I calculate a weighted average?

    Multiply each value by its weight, add those products and divide by the sum of the weights.

    Do weights have to add up to 100?

    No. Any non-negative relative weights work, provided at least one is positive.

    Can a value or weight be negative?

    Values can be negative; weights cannot. A zero weight contributes nothing, and the total weight must be positive.

    What are the limits?

    Enter at most 1,000 pairs, with values from −1 trillion to 1 trillion and weights from zero to 1 billion. Results use floating-point arithmetic and are rounded for display.
