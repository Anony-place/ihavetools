    SIP Calculator

    Calculate the future value of a monthly SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) from your monthly investment, expected annual return and duration — with optional annual step-up, invested amount, estimated gains and a year-by-year growth table.

    Calculations and text processing happen in your browser. Optional share links can contain inputs; see the Privacy Policy.

    How to use SIP Calculator

    1. Enter your monthly investment amount.
    2. Enter an illustrative expected annual return; it is not guaranteed.
    3. Choose the duration and optional annual step-up and read the maturity value and year-by-year growth.

    SIP Calculator guide

    The SIP formula this calculator uses

    Future value = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is your monthly investment, i is the monthly return (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. Each instalment is treated as invested at the start of the month and compounded monthly — When step-up is zero, this is an annuity-due formula. For nonzero step-up, each monthly contribution rises after every 12 deposits.

    Worked example: ₹5,000/month at 12% expected return

    Time in the market does the heavy lifting — compare what the same ₹5,000 a month becomes purely by staying invested longer:

    Read this before trusting any SIP projection

    • Returns are assumed, not guaranteed. Real markets don’t grow a smooth 12% a year — they zig-zag, and your actual result depends on the path.
    • The estimate ignores expense ratios, exit loads, taxes, inflation and market variability. Actual outcomes can be higher or lower.
    • A step-up increases the contribution after every 12 deposits; the amount invested and affordability also increase.
    • Use the output for goal planning (“roughly how much must I invest monthly?”), not as a promise from any fund.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is SIP return calculated?

    Each monthly installment is treated as invested at the start of the month and compounded monthly at one-twelfth of the expected annual return. The maturity value is the sum of all installments plus their compounded growth.

    What return should I assume for equity mutual funds?

    Choose an illustrative assumption and compare scenarios. Returns are uncertain, can be negative, and the calculator’s constant nonnegative rate is not a forecast.

    Is SIP better than a lump sum investment?

    A SIP spreads purchases across market ups and downs (rupee-cost averaging) and suits money you earn monthly. A lump sum invested early often ends higher in rising markets but carries timing risk. The two can also be combined.
