    CGPA Calculator guide

    There is no universal CGPA-to-percentage law

    A CGPA is an average of grade points, while a percentage is based on marks. Two students can hold the same grade point while having different exact marks inside the grade band, so every conversion is an institution’s convention. The ×9.5 preset is widely used for 10-point Indian school results, but your marksheet, transcript or university ordinance is the authority for admissions and job applications.

    SGPA is weighted by credits

    Multiply every subject’s grade point by its credits, add those products, then divide by total credits. Example: grade points 9, 8 and 10 in subjects worth 4, 3 and 2 credits give (36 + 24 + 20) ÷ 9 = 8.89 SGPA. A simple average would give 9.00 and would understate how much the 4-credit subject matters. On a 4-point system, do not assume ×25 unless the institution says so — many use letter-grade bands rather than a linear scale.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is 8.5 CGPA in percentage?

    Using the common 10-point ×9.5 formula, 8.5 CGPA = 8.5 × 9.5 = 80.75%. If your university publishes a different multiplier or grade-band table, that official rule overrides this generic result.

    Why does 10 CGPA become 95% with ×9.5?

    Because 10 × 9.5 is literally 95. The 9.5 factor is an institutional approximation used by bodies such as CBSE to place a grade-point result on an indicative percentage scale; it does not mean a student scored exactly 95 in every subject.

    What is the difference between SGPA and CGPA?

    SGPA is the credit-weighted grade average for one semester. CGPA combines semesters — institutions may weight all subjects by credits across terms or average semester SGPAs under their own rules, so check the academic regulation when combining multiple semesters.
