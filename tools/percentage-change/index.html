    Percentage Change Calculator guide

    Percentage change formula

    Percentage change = ((new value − starting value) ÷ |starting value|) × 100. The sign carries the direction: positive is an increase, negative is a decrease. The absolute starting value in the denominator keeps the direction intuitive when a signed quantity (for example, profit) starts below zero, although percentage changes from a negative base should always be interpreted carefully.

    Going back is not the mirror image

    An increase from 80 to 100 is +25%, but the return journey from 100 to 80 is −20%, because the same 20-unit movement is measured against a larger base. This is why investment losses hurt disproportionately: a 50% fall needs a 100% gain just to break even. The calculator now shows both directions so the effect is visible instead of hidden.

    Three cases that catch people out

    • Starting from zero: percentage change is undefined, because division by the starting value is impossible. Describe “0 to 50” as an increase of 50 units, not as infinity percent.
    • Percentage points: 4% → 5% is +1 percentage point but +25% in relative terms. Interest-rate and election reporting often mixes these up.
    • Successive changes multiply, they do not add: +10% followed by +10% is +21% overall (1.10 × 1.10 = 1.21), not +20%.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I calculate percentage increase?

    Subtract the starting value from the new value, divide by the starting value, then multiply by 100. From 80 to 100: (100 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase. For a decrease the same formula produces a negative result.

    Why does a 50% fall need a 100% rise to recover?

    Because the base changes. Falling from 100 to 50 is −50%, but returning from 50 to 100 means gaining 50 on a base of 50, which is +100%. The reverse-change figure above shows this asymmetry for your numbers.

    What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

    A rate moving from 4% to 5% has risen by 1 percentage point, but by 25% relative to its starting value ((5 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100). Percentage points describe the arithmetic gap between two percentages; percentage change describes the proportional change.
