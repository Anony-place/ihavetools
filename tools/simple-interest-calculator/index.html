    Simple Interest Calculator guide

    The simple interest formula

    Interest = P × R × T ÷ 100 — principal × annual rate × time in years, divided by 100. Example: ₹50,000 at 8% for 3 years earn 50,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = ₹12,000 interest, ₹62,000 in total. The defining feature: interest is always calculated on the original principal only, so it grows in a straight line — the same ₹4,000 every year in this example.

    Simple vs compound interest on the same money

    In year one they are identical — there is no past interest to compound yet. The gap then widens every year, which is why simple interest only survives in short-duration products: over long periods it badly short-changes the lender (or, flipped around, overcharges a borrower on flat-rate loans).

    Converting months and days

    The T in the formula is always in years, so months are divided by 12 and days by 365 — this calculator does that conversion for you when you pick a unit. Rent deposits, short bridges between payments and penalty interest are the common cases where the day-count version shows up.

    Frequently asked questions

    Where is simple interest actually used?

    Short-term informal loans, some car and consumer loans quoted at “flat” rates, late-payment penalties, and bonds that pay interest out instead of reinvesting it. Bank deposits and most formal loans use compound (reducing-balance) interest instead.

    Why does a “flat rate” loan cost more than it sounds?

    Because flat/simple interest is charged on the full original principal for the whole tenure, even though you repay part of it every month. A 10% flat rate works out to roughly double that as an effective reducing-balance rate over typical tenures.
