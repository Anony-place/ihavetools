    The rules (and one worked example)

    • Symbols are added left to right, largest first: MMXXVI = 1000+1000+10+10+5+1 = 2026.
    • I, X, C and M may repeat up to 3 times (III = 3); V, L and D never repeat (10 is X, never VV).
    • Subtraction is allowed only in six pairs — IV (4), IX (9), XL (40), XC (90), CD (400), CM (900) — always one smaller symbol directly before a larger one.
    • Worked example: 1994 = 1000 + 900 + 90 + 4 = M + CM + XC + IV = MCMXCIV.

    Limits of the system

    Roman numerals have no zero and no fractions, and the standard form tops out at 3999 (MMMCMXCIX). Medieval writers drew a line (vinculum) over a numeral to multiply it by 1000 for larger values — you will see it in old manuscripts, but modern usage (chapters, monarchs like Charles III, Super Bowl numbering, copyright years) never needs it. That 1–3999 range is what this converter enforces.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why is 49 written XLIX and not IL?

    A smaller symbol may only be subtracted from the next one or two larger symbols: I subtracts only from V and X, X only from L and C, C only from D and M. So 49 = 40 + 9 = XL + IX = XLIX. IL breaks the rule (I cannot subtract from L).

    How do you write the current year in Roman numerals?

    2026 = MMXXVI (2000 = MM, 20 = XX, 6 = VI). Type any year above to check — film credits and building cornerstones still use this every year.

    Is IIII ever correct for 4?

    In the standard subtractive system, no — 4 is IV. But many traditional clock and watch faces deliberately use IIII for visual balance opposite VIII, a centuries-old exception you will spot on tower clocks.
