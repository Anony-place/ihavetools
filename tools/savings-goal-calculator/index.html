    Savings Goal Calculator

    Plan monthly savings for a target using your current balance, timeline and an editable assumed return, with growth and contribution breakdown.

    Calculations and text processing happen in your browser. Optional share links can contain inputs; see the Privacy Policy.

    How to use Savings Goal Calculator

    1. Enter the target amount, current savings and months remaining.
    2. Set an assumed annual return or use zero for a no-growth budget.
    3. Calculate the monthly end-of-month contribution and projected balance.

    Savings Goal Calculator guide

    Monthly savings at a constant return

    The initial balance compounds monthly at annual rate ÷ 12; each new contribution arrives at the end of a month. The tool solves for the equal monthly contribution that reaches the target. It uses your chosen currency consistently without assuming a particular investment.

    A simple zero-return example

    For a 100,000 target with 10,000 already saved and 12 months to go, the gap is 90,000. With no return, 90,000 ÷ 12 = 7,500 per month. Shortening the deadline raises the monthly amount; a longer deadline reduces it.

    Use projections responsibly

    • Try a zero-return case as well as a positive return; investment returns are not guaranteed.
    • Include a buffer for inflation, tax and costs that the calculation does not model.
    • Money needed soon may require a different risk level from a long-term goal.
    • The calculator uses end-of-month deposits; deposits at the beginning of the month can produce a different result.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the return guaranteed?

    No. The rate is a user-chosen constant assumption, not a guarantee. Actual returns may vary or be negative, and fees and tax can reduce the amount available.

    What if I already have enough saved?

    If the projected initial savings alone meet the target, the required new contribution is zero. The final balance may exceed the target.

    Does this account for inflation?

    No. Enter an inflation-adjusted future target yourself if the cost of your goal may increase, and consider taxes, fees and risk separately.
