    Age Calculator guide

    How exact age is calculated

    Your age is counted in completed units: full years first, then full months since your last birthday-month, then the remaining days. When the current day-of-month is smaller than your birth day, days are “borrowed” from the previous month — which is why the result depends on whether that month had 28, 30 or 31 days. This tool also adds everything up into total days, weeks and hours, and counts down to your next birthday.

    Where exact age actually matters

    • School admissions: many boards require a child to be a certain age “as on” a cut-off date (often 31 December or 31 March) — a few days can shift the admission year.
    • Government exams and jobs: eligibility is checked against a fixed “age as on” date printed in the notification, not the day you fill the form.
    • Retirement and service records: pension and gratuity calculations run on exact years/months/days of age and service.
    • Forms that ask for age in years-months-days rather than just a date of birth — copy the breakdown straight from the result above.

    Leap years and your total days

    Every February 29 you have lived through adds an extra day to your total. Someone born on 29 February 2000, for example, has lived 9,741 days by 31 October 2026 (26 years, 8 months, 2 days) — including 6 leap days since their birth. It’s also why two people with the same age in years can differ by a day in total days lived.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I calculate age on a specific date, not just today?

    Yes — set the optional “age at date” field. This is what exam and job forms mean by “age as on 1 January”: your completed years/months/days on that exact date, which can decide eligibility.

    Why does my exact age in months/days sometimes look one day off?

    Because months have different lengths. Exact age counts completed years, then completed months, then the leftover days — borrowing days from the previous month when needed. Leap years (an extra day every 4 years) are counted in your total days.

    I was born on 29 February. When do I officially get older?

    It depends on the form and country — many treat 1 March as the birthday in non-leap years, others use 28 February. For eligibility forms, use whichever date the form’s rules specify; your total completed years still increase once every year.
