    How to use Discount Calculator

    1. Enter the original price and the discount percentage.

    3. Read the final price and how much you save.

    Discount Calculator guide

    Successive discounts don’t add — they multiply

    Two stacked offers combine as a + b − (a × b ÷ 100), because the second discount acts on the price left after the first. “50% + extra 10%” = 50 + 10 − 5 = 55% off: on ₹2,000 you pay ₹900 and save ₹1,100, as the step table above shows. Three offers stack the same way — apply the formula pairwise. A single honest 55% off would be identical; the stacked version just sounds bigger.

    Sale-season traps worth 10 seconds of math

    • Inflated MRP: “70% off” on an MRP nobody ever charged is not a saving. Compare the final price against other sellers, not against the struck-out number.
    • “Up to 70% off”: the headline rate may apply to a handful of items. Judge the item in your cart by its own final price.
    • Buy-2-get-1: that is 33.3% off only if you genuinely needed all three at equal prices — otherwise it is a way to make you spend more, not less.
    • Coupon caps: “20% off up to ₹150” quietly becomes a shrinking percentage as your bill grows — on ₹3,000 it is just 5%.

    Discount vs markup — the seller’s side of the same coin

    A shop that buys at ₹600 and marks up 25% prices at ₹750; a “20% off” sale on ₹750 brings it to ₹600 — back to cost, not below. Markup is calculated on cost, discount on selling price, so equal percentages do not cancel out: +25% then −20% returns exactly to the start (as does any +x% followed by −x÷(1+x)%). Knowing this pair makes both sale tags and salary-hike percentages much easier to read.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is “50% + extra 10% off” the same as 60% off?

    No. The extra 10% applies to the already-reduced price: ₹2,000 → 50% off → ₹1,000 → 10% off → ₹900. That is 55% off in total, not 60%. Combined discount = a + b − (a × b ÷ 100) = 50 + 10 − 5 = 55%.

    Is tax calculated before or after the discount?

    After. Tax (like GST) applies to the price actually charged, so a discount also reduces the tax you pay. This calculator follows that order: discounts first, then tax on the discounted price.
